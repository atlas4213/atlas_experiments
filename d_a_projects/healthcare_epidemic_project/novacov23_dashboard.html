Analysis Report — NovaCov-23 Outbreak

PREPARED BY: JUNIOR ANALYST · MILLBROOK COUNTY HEALTH DISTRICT · JANUARY 2024 · DRAFT v1.2

Overview

This report documents the NovaCov-23 outbreak across the Millbrook County Health District during FY 2023 (January 1 – December 31). Data was extracted from the district's case reporting system and covers 20,138 confirmed cases across 7 counties following data cleaning and validation. The year was characterised by three distinct epidemic waves, four circulating variants, and an evolving vaccination landscape. The case fatality rate (CFR) for the full year was 1.93%, resulting in 388 deaths. Total hospitalisations reached 3,895 (19.3% of cases).

Epidemic Waves

Three epidemic waves were identified using rolling weekly case counts. Wave 1 peaked around Week 9 (early March) with the Original variant dominant. Wave 2 was the most severe, peaking at Week 32 (mid-August) with 819 cases in a single week, coinciding with the Delta-X variant surge. Wave 3 began building in late November and continued through December, driven by Omicron-Z. The lowest case counts were observed in May and October — inter-wave troughs consistent with respiratory illness seasonality.

📊 Note: Wave 2 peak (Wk 32) was approximately 4× the Wave 1 peak. Worth flagging to the epidemiology team whether this reflects true transmission differences or reporting delays catching up.

Geographic Risk

Urban counties — Maplewood (8,438 cases) and Riverside (6,179 cases) — accounted for 72.6% of total case volume by population density. However, rural counties showed consistently higher clinical severity. Pinegrove had the highest CFR at 3.87% and Laketon had the highest hospitalisation rate at 23.6%. This rural severity disparity likely reflects a combination of older demographics, longer distances to acute care, and lower baseline health system capacity.

Age Group Vulnerability

The 65+ age group had a 38.5% hospitalisation rate and 5.35% CFR — both dramatically higher than any other cohort. For comparison, the 0–17 group had a 3.5% hospitalisation rate and 0.11% CFR. The 18–34 and 35–49 groups carried a surprisingly similar burden (~16–17% hosp rate), suggesting working-age adults had meaningful exposure, possibly occupational. Average hospital length of stay for the 65+ group was 3.0 days vs 0.3 days for 0–17.

📊 Observation: The similarity in hospitalisation rates between 18–34 and 35–49 was unexpected. Worth investigating whether this reflects occupational exposure patterns or comorbidity differences — would need additional data fields to confirm.

Variant Analysis

Four variants circulated during 2023. The Original strain and Delta-X showed similar and higher severity (CFR ~2.6%), while Omicron-Y and Omicron-Z showed meaningfully lower CFRs of 1.22% and 1.13% respectively — consistent with globally observed Omicron attenuation. Omicron-Y was the most prevalent variant by case count (7,053 cases, 35% of total). The shift toward milder variants in the second half of the year partially explains why Wave 3 case volumes were rising while CFR was declining.

Vaccine Effectiveness

Vaccination coverage grew from approximately 15% in January to 65.8% by December. Vaccinated individuals showed a 1.3pp lower hospitalisation rate (18.7% vs 20.0%) and a 0.57pp lower CFR (1.64% vs 2.21%) compared to unvaccinated cases. While statistically meaningful, the effect size appears modest in aggregate — this is likely because the dataset mixes all age groups together and the protective effect is strongest in the 65+ cohort. A stratified analysis by age group would provide a clearer picture of vaccine effectiveness.

Recommendations

01
Prioritise rural surge capacity: Pinegrove and Laketon show disproportionate severity despite low case volumes. Pre-positioning resources before Wave 3 peaks could reduce preventable deaths.
02
Targeted 65+ vaccination push: Coverage data suggests the age group with the highest risk may still have room for improved vaccination rates. A stratified effectiveness analysis should inform campaign targeting.
03
Investigate working-age hospitalisation: The 18–49 group hospitalisation rate warrants further investigation. Occupational exposure data could identify high-risk worker categories for targeted intervention.
04
Prepare for Wave 3: Omicron-Z is driving a December resurgence. While less fatal, high case volumes still stress hospital capacity. Early warning thresholds should be set based on Wave 2 data.

Data Limitations

Several caveats apply to this analysis. First, the dataset does not include comorbidity data, which limits our ability to fully explain the rural severity gap or working-age hospitalisation patterns. Second, vaccination status is self-reported in some county systems and may under-count coverage. Third, the 4 dirty rows removed during cleaning (duplicate, impossible date, invalid categoricals) represent a tiny fraction of the dataset and are unlikely to affect conclusions, but are documented in the cleaning script for full transparency. A follow-up analysis including comorbidities and socioeconomic indicators would strengthen the recommendations significantly.
